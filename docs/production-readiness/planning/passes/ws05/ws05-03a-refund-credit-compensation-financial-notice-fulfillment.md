# WS05-03A — Refund, credit, compensation, and financial-notice fulfillment

Make each known financial obligation recoverable and value preserving, and tell a customer that value was returned only after the corresponding outcome commits.

## 1. What This Work Does

This work covers the monetary obligations already created by official-game checkout, cancellation, and player removal; community publish-fee decisions; and admin money repair. A successful Stripe payment without a booking entitlement already creates a PaymentCompensation row, but that row does not yet cause a refund. Other refund paths can call Stripe before their local refund record commits, leaving a crash or timeout difficult to reconcile safely.

The result is one active recoverable Stripe refund attempt per authorized refund intent, transactionally safe local credit returns, and financial notices based on completed value return. It uses the existing Refund, RefundEvent, GameCreditUsage, AdminFinancialOutcome, MoneyIssue, notification, Stripe client, and durable-job systems. Existing preview, approval, authorization, credit eligibility, booking entitlement, and manual money-repair rules remain in force.

This work handles obligations whose origin is already known. Periodic discovery of unresolved or recent monetary state, final worker hosting and runtime proof, and deployed Stripe verification are outside this implementation. Ordinary in-app notice rows stay in the same database transaction as the outcome they report.

## 2. What Must Be True

These are the observable financial and notification rules. They define success because a refund can be retried, observed out of order, or overlap another refund while the user and staff still need an accurate account of what was returned.

### 2.1 Value and obligation identity

- A late successful payment with no booking entitlement produces one lifetime compensation obligation for that payment and booking. Its refund intents reserve only collected cash not already confirmed or reserved for return; the obligation completes only when the full collected amount is confirmed returned. Credit is not restored a second time. Replayed payment events cannot create another obligation or another full refund.
- Official-game cancellation and player removal preserve their existing eligibility, preview, credit release or restoration, and booking or roster effects. Each refundable Stripe payment receives at most its remaining refundable amount. A failed credit operation follows the existing rollback or MoneyIssue rule for its originating workflow; it never silently grants credit.
- A community publish-fee refund returns cash only after Stripe confirms it; a publish-fee credit creates the existing HostPublishEntitlement exactly once in the local transaction. A GameCredit return and a publish entitlement are distinct forms of value.
- A pending publish-fee manual review has an authorized idempotent path to exactly one valid final replacement. A later authoritative publish-fee decision cannot leave any earlier failed or cancelled cash-refund issue recommending another cash refund. Every active sibling decision blocks every earlier Refund retry; staff can resolve those issues as superseded only after a verified final replacement.
- For every payment, current pending, approved, and processing attempt reservations plus one confirmed returned amount per succeeded attempt never intentionally exceed collected cash. Provider-unknown work remains processing and reserved. A late historical success is counted even if it reveals an over-refund and immediately blocks further mutation. Distinct authorized partial refunds remain possible. A definite failed or cancelled provider attempt can be retried only after its outcome is confirmed; an unknown attempt cannot be replaced with a new Stripe mutation.
- Payment.payment_status remains the truth of the collected Stripe payment. Booking.payment_status and HostPublishFee.fee_status reflect confirmed refund totals, not refund intent or a single payment when a booking has multiple payments.

### 2.2 Provider outcome and recovery

- A provider-call-capable local refund attempt commits its identity, amount, charge, approval, and durable work item before Stripe is asked to create a refund. A locally detected missing charge instead commits a definite no-call failed Refund and MoneyIssue without a job. Both forms use the same local attempt and request-key identity rules. Replays, worker crashes, timeouts, rate limits, and duplicate jobs reuse the same operation identity until a definite outcome permits a new attempt.
- The durable refund job's payload, protected identity, key, and origin reference identify the same Refund and attempt. An exact enqueue replay returns its original job; a conflicting replay fails before any provider call.
- Provider responses, refund webhooks, targeted reads, and authorized admin reconciliation apply the same validated, monotonic refund transition and the same normalization of every Stripe Refund status. A stale event cannot undo a confirmed success or attach a refund to the wrong payment, charge, amount, currency, or attempt.
- Every provider observation records the immutable number, amount, currency, key, and provider identity of the attempt it describes. A verified older-attempt success counts as returned cash and blocks newer mutation until authorized reconciliation proves the remaining obligation and every attempt outcome.
- An unresolved provider outcome stays visibly processing or unknown, with the amount reserved. Recovery reads Stripe before any repeat mutation and stops automatic mutation when the original request identity can no longer be relied on. Exhausted or unsafe obligations surface through the existing MoneyIssue path or, if issue staging itself fails, an explicit admin-visible exhausted-job diagnostic.
- A definitive failure or cancellation does not count as returned value. Existing admin refund retry and reconcile actions remain usable and auditable; a retry cannot evade the amount reservation or create a second refund for the same obligation.

### 2.3 Notices and interfaces

- The booking payer receives a booking refund notice only for cash confirmed refunded or credit actually released or restored. Its cash, released-credit, restored-credit, mixed-credit, or combined wording reflects the components that have completed at that moment. A later component updates or reopens the same booking notice without duplicate delivery.
- A host receives a publish-fee refund notice only after the refund succeeds, or a publish-credit notice only after the entitlement commits. Pending, failed, cancelled, and unknown cash outcomes never produce success wording.
- Admin money records, compensation state, audit events, and existing API responses distinguish work awaiting a worker, work underway, a confirmed return, a definite failure, and an unknown provider outcome using their existing separate status fields. Both checkout payment-intent and checkout-status reads return the lifetime PaymentCompensation row for their selected payment and booking in required, processing, succeeded, or failed state; a terminal row is never hidden as a null compensation status. Cancellation and removal responses report a queued provider-capable intent as approved and report processing only after the worker checkpoint; their UI must not label either as returned cash. Every actual refund-derived or credit-derived Booking.payment_status change appends its accepted BookingStatusHistory transition in the same transaction. Existing active-admin and step-up requirements continue to protect repair actions.

## 3. Design

The implementation commits the reason and exact amount of a refund before an external mutation, then lets a focused durable worker complete or reconcile that operation. Local credit changes and notices remain synchronous database work. A single refund outcome function keeps immediate provider observations, webhook delivery, and staff repair from diverging.

### 3.1 Stable refund intent and amount accounting

Give Refund a non-null, unique origin operation key; a non-null current attempt number; a unique, nullable Stripe request key; a nullable attempt-started timestamp; and nullable `automatic_mutation_blocked_reason` and `automatic_mutation_blocked_at`. The reason vocabulary contains only `historical_attempt_conflict` in this pass, and a database check requires the reason and timestamp to be either both null or both non-null. Every locally created Refund begins at attempt number 1, and each later authorized retry increments that number. Every positive attempt receives the deterministic Stripe request key `refund:<normalized Refund UUID>:attempt:<positive integer>`, derived by the service after the Refund UUID and attempt number are fixed and never accepted from a request. A provider-call-capable attempt commits the approved Refund and its job together, and its Stripe request key equals the durable-job idempotency key. A missing charge proved during origin processing commits the same local attempt number and derived request key, records a local_status_changed RefundEvent with that attempt snapshot, records the Refund as failed with a null started timestamp, and creates no job because no provider execution is possible. A preflight failure discovered by an already-enqueued worker retains that job and completes it according to the job-result mapping. The started timestamp stays null until the worker commits its pre-call checkpoint.

Enforce the attempt/key shape in PostgreSQL: an imported attempt-zero row requires `current_attempt_number = 0`, a null request key, and a non-null verified provider refund ID; every local row requires a positive attempt number and a request key equal to the expression derived from its Refund UUID and current attempt number. The service invariant requiring a matching job applies only while a local attempt is approved or processing and eligible for provider execution; definite origin-time no-call failures have no job. The origin key identifies one business obligation, not merely a payment: cancellation uses game and payment; player removal uses the booking party and payment; a publish-fee decision uses its financial outcome; compensation uses its compensation row. A retained direct/admin Refund uses `direct_admin_refund:refund:<normalized Refund UUID>`; its UUID is allocated before insert, and retired generic mutation paths cannot create another direct/admin obligation. Remove the unused RefundCreate and RefundUpdate schema exports and the create_refund_record and update_refund_record production services. Tests that need a retained direct/admin record use a fixture helper that allocates the Refund UUID and exact origin key together and cannot accept or mutate state-machine-owned fields through a generic service.

For player removal, use `player_removal:booking:<booking UUID>:payment:<payment UUID>` with normalized UUID strings. The booking-party removal is a one-time obligation, and the booking and payment IDs exist before refund intent creation. Repeated execution under the same booking/payment locks finds the same Refund by origin key; a different selected participant in that booking does not create a second refund. The later admin_remove_player AdminAction links to the resulting Refund for audit and is never the origin identity. The request's preview token or a newly generated AdminAction UUID cannot substitute for this stable key.

The nullable request key exists only for an older Stripe refund discovered from a validated webhook that has a provider refund ID but no recoverable original request key. For the recoverable official-game cancellation case, both new local intents and legacy imports use origin key `official_game_cancellation:game:<game UUID>:payment:<payment UUID>` with normalized UUID strings. Validate the legacy Stripe source and game, booking, and payment UUID metadata against the linked local rows; also validate charge, the original full-payment cancellation amount, and currency before import. The provider refund ID remains the provider identity, never the business-origin key. Look up that origin key before inserting so a later cancellation replay finds the imported Refund. If the canonical identity or monetary match cannot be proved, do not invent a Refund row; retain the failed provider event for staff reconciliation. An imported row has attempt number 0 and cannot issue another Stripe mutation until an authorized retry establishes a positive attempt and key. The canonical migration rebuilds pre-production databases; it does not backfill arbitrary old local rows.

Keep RefundEvent as the sparse attempt and observation history, with one exact immutable snapshot contract. Add non-null `attempt_number`, `attempt_amount_cents`, and `attempt_currency` columns plus nullable `attempt_request_key`; require a nonnegative attempt number, positive cents, `attempt_currency = 'USD'`, a null key for attempt zero, and the exact derived key for every positive attempt. Every event writer copies those values from the attempt it describes, alongside the existing provider refund and charge identity, before writing the event. Event metadata remains bounded context and is not the source of attempt amount or currency truth. Historical lookup by provider refund ID must validate the event's first-class attempt snapshot before applying an observation. Record the safe reason and prior compensation outcome before an authorized retry clears current-attempt fields. A new attempt on the same Refund row is permitted only when the previous Stripe refund is confirmed failed or cancelled, or a preflight failure proves no Stripe call was made. Name the compensation Refund reason unfulfilled_booking and use origin_workflow official_game_checkout; update the existing model, migration, validation, and read contracts for that exact allowed value.

The current checkout schema enforces at most one active compensation per payment and booking and retains terminal rows. Here the same collected charge without entitlement is one lasting obligation, so replace the active-only index with lifetime uniqueness on payment plus booking and look up every status on replay. This strengthens the existing active invariant and prevents a succeeded or failed row from being followed by another full refund obligation. A failed row is reactivated only by authorized repair after provider proof. Before reactivation, its prior outcome remains in RefundEvent and MoneyIssueEvent, with the admin action identifying the retry. No compensation row is deleted.

PaymentCompensation has exactly four statuses: required, processing, succeeded, and failed. Remove the unused cancelled status from its model and canonical migration check constraint and from any finite validation or read contract. A successful collected payment without entitlement still owes value until its cash is fully returned; there is no valid transition that cancels that obligation. A Stripe Refund that is cancelled makes its linked compensation failed and repairable, never compensation cancelled. The booking_cancelled compensation reason remains valid and is distinct from compensation status.

Update both checkout projections to select the lifetime-unique PaymentCompensation by the selected Payment ID plus Booking ID without filtering to active statuses. If no selected payment exists, there is no compensation projection. Return required, processing, succeeded, or failed through the existing compensation_status field; never choose an older payment's compensation merely because it shares the booking. Remove cancelled from schema examples and finite frontend/read vocabularies.

A compensation row points to its fulfilling Refund where one is needed. Under the payment lock, first count confirmed and reserved refunds. If cash is already fully returned, mark compensation succeeded without another Stripe call. If another origin has an active reservation, keep compensation required until that refund reaches a definite outcome; this prevents overlapping residual intents. Then create one compensation intent for the still-unreturned amount. Compensation succeeds only when the payment's confirmed refunds cover its collected amount. The compensation amount remains the full collected obligation even if its linked Refund is only the remainder. The payment must still be succeeded and the booking must still lack the entitlement before creating this obligation.

Re-evaluate every failed or cancelled retryable Refund whenever another attempt or origin for its Payment reaches a definite outcome. The rule applies to compensation, cancellation, removal, and retained direct/admin Refunds; publish-fee retries also obey their stricter sibling-decision rule. Under the payment lock, compute confirmed returned cash from unique succeeded attempt snapshots and active reservations from current attempts. If another reservation exists, leave the issue open and defer retry. If no unreturned cash remains, create no attempt or job, change the issue recommendation to review_and_resolve_no_action, and permit the verified no-action resolution below while retaining the failed Refund history. If a positive partial remainder remains and the prior attempt definitely returned no value, update the same Refund's amount to that remainder, preserve the old attempt amount in RefundEvent, update the open MoneyIssue to the same cents and currency, and create the next keyed attempt. Append RefundEvent and MoneyIssueEvent rows recording the old and new amounts and attempt number; the MoneyIssueEvent references the retry AdminAction. This rule never shrinks or replaces an unresolved attempt.

Apply the same recount to compensation state. A required compensation creates its single intent after other active reservations clear; a failed compensation with a linked Refund waits for authorized repair instead of silently creating another row. Confirmed returned cash counts toward the full collected compensation obligation, including a succeeded attempt belonging to another origin. Mark compensation succeeded without another call once the confirmed attempt ledger covers the charge. The compensation amount remains the full collected obligation even when its linked Refund contains only the remaining amount. The compensation link is a nullable Refund foreign key; the earlier Refund migration already precedes the compensation migration.

Extend the existing MoneyIssue resolution validator for payment-level no-action outcomes. retried_successfully still requires its target Refund's current attempt to have succeeded. provider_completed_no_action_required may resolve an issue whose target Refund remains failed or cancelled when a locked payment-level recount proves no refundable cash remains for that obligation and no current or historical attempt for the payment remains capable of returning additional value. A linked compensation must also be succeeded for a compensation issue; a publish-fee issue must satisfy the sibling replacement rules below. The current resolver locks MoneyIssue first, so this rule first reads context without a row lock, acquires any required booking lock, then Payment, Refund and dependent financial rows, and finally MoneyIssue, and revalidates identity, open status, idempotency key, returned cash, reservations, and attempt history before committing the resolution and audit. Any missing link, remaining cash, active reservation, or unresolved attempt leaves the issue open; recommendation text alone never authorizes resolution.

All refund-intent producers serialize on the Payment row before calculating available cash and inserting a Refund. For attempts without contradictory history, the reservation predicate is exactly current refund_status in pending, approved, or processing; confirmed return comes from one succeeded RefundEvent snapshot per `(refund_id, attempt_number)`, counted once even if duplicate provider events exist. A normal succeeded Refund therefore contributes its current attempt snapshot rather than being counted again from the Refund row. This attempt ledger is necessary because a verified older attempt can succeed after the Refund row advances to a later attempt. Failed and cancelled attempts free their amount only when definitely unsuccessful. Hold the existing game or booking locks first when an originating workflow needs them, followed by payment and refund locks; never acquire game or booking locks after financial locks. A unique origin key closes replay races, and the payment lock closes overlap between different origins. Neither a database lock nor a transaction remains open across a Stripe call.

A validated success for an older attempt is returned cash and cannot be treated as history only. In the same payment/refund transaction that records it, set the Refund's `automatic_mutation_blocked_reason` to `historical_attempt_conflict` and `automatic_mutation_blocked_at`, count that attempt snapshot in payment, booking, compensation, and publish-fee returned-value calculations, and stage `refund_outcome_unknown` with `review_unknown_outcome`. The worker rechecks the block while holding the Refund lock in its pre-call checkpoint. If the newer attempt is still approved with a null started timestamp, mark that unstarted attempt failed with a no-call conflict reason, release its reservation, and complete its job as handled without calling Stripe. If the newer attempt has a started timestamp, treat it as potentially invoked: keep it processing and reserved, exhaust automatic work, and surface the possible excess until both attempts are reconciled. The checkpoint is the safety boundary; a conflict arriving after it is handled as potentially concurrent invocation even if no response has committed.

Only step-up-protected reconciliation may clear this block. It must read and validate every known attempt for the Refund, record any additional observations through the same transition service, recompute unique succeeded attempt cash and unresolved reservations, and then either resolve the obligation with no new mutation or authorize only the remaining positive amount under a new attempt and key. The block remains set while any attempt is unknown or the successful historical value has not been incorporated. No worker, retry, webhook, or issue recommendation may bypass it.

For a publish-fee refund, the row-lock order is an existing worker job row when applicable, then Payment, HostPublishFee, Refund, AdminFinancialOutcome, and MoneyIssue. The initiating admin action first reads the fee's payment ID without a row lock, then locks Payment before HostPublishFee and revalidates their association, amount, status, and active decision. Worker, webhook, admin retry/reconcile, and financial-outcome paths read IDs without locking first, then acquire the same ordered rows and revalidate. In particular, replace the current fee-before-payment origin lock and refund-before-payment webhook/retry locks; no Stripe call holds any of these locks. Paths needing a game or booking lock take it before this financial order.

Align models and their canonical Alembic table migrations with these fields, constraints, indexes, and the new refund reason. Use the project's clean-rebuild migration policy; do not add a patch migration for existing tables. Preserve the accepted refund webhook's safe handling of a provider refund that predates the new request metadata, while refusing to infer an unverified Stripe outcome.

### 3.2 Refund and dependent state transitions

These mappings prevent an interface from treating a queued operation, a possible Stripe call, and confirmed returned cash as the same state. Refund.refund_status is the local state; Refund.provider_status is a separate observation and has no queued value. Related-state entries apply only when that Refund has the named compensation or financial-outcome link.

| Event | Refund fields | Related state |
|---|---|---|
| Provider-call-capable local intent or definite-failure retry | approved; provider_status and provider_refund_id null; attempt number and request key committed; attempt-started and provider-observed timestamps null; refunded_at null; approved_at set | Durable job pending with exactly six maximum attempts; compensation required; refund financial outcome pending with applied_at null |
| Origin processing proves the charge is missing | failed; provider_status and provider-refund ID null; positive local attempt number and derived request key committed; attempt-started and refunded_at null | No durable job; compensation or refund financial outcome failed as applicable; MoneyIssue opened |
| Worker commits the pre-call checkpoint | processing; provider_status still null; attempt-started timestamp set | Job leased; compensation processing |
| Stripe reports raw pending | processing; provider_status processing; validated provider ID and observation time set; RefundEvent safe reason stripe_refund_pending | Compensation processing; amount reserved |
| Stripe reports raw requires_action | processing; provider_status processing; validated provider ID and observation time set; RefundEvent safe reason stripe_refund_requires_action; no raw next_action retained | Compensation processing; amount reserved; observation-only recovery and staff review if bounded reads do not settle it |
| Call, read, or lease outcome is ambiguous | processing; provider_status unknown; observation time set when there is an actual provider/error observation; provider ID retained if known | Compensation processing; amount reserved; MoneyIssue when recovery becomes unsafe or exhausted |
| Stripe confirms success | succeeded; provider_status succeeded; provider ID, observation time, and refunded_at set | Compensation succeeded only when full payment cash is confirmed returned; refund financial outcome applied with applied_at; booking/fee summaries and notice updated |
| Stripe definitively fails or cancels | failed or cancelled; matching provider_status and observation time; refunded_at null | Compensation failed; refund financial outcome failed with applied_at; MoneyIssue opened; amount no longer reserved |
| Preflight proves no provider call can start | failed; provider_status and provider-refund ID null; attempt-started and refunded_at null | Compensation or refund financial outcome failed as applicable; MoneyIssue opened |

The model also allows Refund pending, but this is an unapproved local record with no provider job; it still reserves its amount. New provider-call-capable origins create approved rows directly, while a definite origin-time missing-charge result uses failed. Refund cancelled remains a valid definite provider outcome and maps linked compensation to failed. Non-refund financial outcomes keep their existing applied or not_applicable rules. Queued is a display description of an approved Refund with a pending job, not an additional persisted enum; unknown is provider_status unknown while the local Refund remains processing.

For linked compensation, required has null processing_started_at and resolved_at. The worker checkpoint sets processing_started_at; a confirmed success or definite failure sets resolved_at and clears or sets a safe error_code. An ambiguous provider outcome keeps processing and a safe error_code without a resolved_at. For a linked refund financial outcome, pending has null applied_at; success or definite failure sets it. The approving admin remains the actor on the decision; the worker is never represented as an admin.

On authorized retry, lock and recheck the payment reservation, preserve the prior provider result in RefundEvent, increment the attempt number, derive and commit a new request key, clear the current provider ID/status/observation and attempt-started timestamps, reset refunded_at, and set Refund approved. Keep its original requested_at and approved_at; record the new actor and approval in AdminAction and RefundEvent. Reset linked failed compensation to required, clearing its processing_started_at, resolved_at, and error_code after recording the previous values. Reset linked failed refund financial outcome to pending, clearing its failure_reason, applied_at, and applied_by_user_id. Pending means no application has occurred; the retrying admin is identified by the immutable AdminAction and RefundEvent, not by applied_by_user_id. A retry is never allowed from succeeded or an ambiguous processing state.

### 3.3 Origin workflows and local credits

On a late paid checkout without entitlement, the webhook or targeted payment reconciliation writes the compensation, any eligible remaining provider-call-capable Refund intent, and its durable job in the same transaction as the payment transition. It does not call Stripe there. The compensation stays required while another refund reserves cash, processing while its own provider result is pending, succeeded only after full confirmed cash return, and failed only after a definite failure or a preflight that proves no mutation can start. A missing charge creates a linked local attempt-one failed Refund with its derived request key and MoneyIssue but no job; an authorized retry reactivates that same compensation after the charge is available. An unknown result remains processing and opens MoneyIssue when automatic recovery is unsafe or exhausted.

For official-game cancellation and player removal, preserve the existing authorized preview and credit ledger steps. Commit the cancellation or removal, successful local credit release or restoration, each approved cash-refund intent, and the corresponding jobs together. A local credit failure retains the workflow's existing abort or explicit repair behavior. A missing provider charge creates a visible local attempt-one failed Refund with its derived key and MoneyIssue but no mutation job. Repeated requests find the same origin intent and do not return value twice. The response exposes an enqueued provider-capable Refund as approved and exposes processing only after the worker checkpoint. Keep refund_created_count as the compatibility total, add refund_approved_count for queued work, and restrict refund_processing_count to attempts whose checkpoint committed. Per-booking results expose the same persisted status. The UI labels approved as queued or pending fulfillment and never persists pending merely as a display translation.

Enforce the publish-fee decision matrix in the service, independently of the UI. A paid fee backed by its collected community_publish_fee Payment permits refund, credit, forfeit, or manual_review. An unpaid or uncollected fee permits only no_fee_charged or manual_review. Refund, credit, and forfeit are full-fee decisions: amount_cents must equal HostPublishFee.amount_cents and the payment, host, payment type, currency, paid_at, and succeeded payment state must match the fee. no_fee_charged requires no collected payment and stores zero applied value; reject it for a collected fee. manual_review is non-settling: it may identify the full fee under review but cannot return value, release a reservation, complete the fee, or resolve an older refund issue. Reject zero, partial, excessive, mismatched, and invalid state/outcome combinations before inserting the outcome or AdminAction.

Give manual_review one finite completion path instead of leaving its pending active decision permanent. Add `superseded` to AdminFinancialOutcome.applied_status and add nullable `superseded_at`; the canonical check requires that status to have superseded_at set while applied_at and applied_by_user_id remain null, and all other statuses require superseded_at null. Superseded is terminal and excluded from the active-fee unique index. Preserve replacement linkage in the immutable AdminAction metadata rather than a mutable outcome pointer: the action records both the manual-review outcome ID and the replacement outcome ID.

Add `POST /admin/money/financial-outcomes/{financial_outcome_id}/resolve` as the step-up-protected, idempotent operation addressed to a pending manual-review outcome. Its narrow request contains outcome, reason, optional internal note, amount, and the required admin idempotency key; the path supplies the reviewed outcome and the service derives fee, host, game, and payment identity from locked rows. It does not expose a generic outcome update. Read the payment ID, then lock Payment, HostPublishFee, the manual-review outcome, related failed Refunds and issues in the established order, and revalidate that the target is the fee's sole active pending manual review. Allocate the replacement outcome and AdminAction IDs, mark the manual review superseded, create and validate the replacement decision, and record a `resolve_manual_review` AdminAction in one transaction; add that action to the finite audit vocabulary. Return the replacement through AdminMoneyFinancialOutcomeRead and add superseded_at to that read schema. A repeated key returns the same replacement; a different replacement under that key conflicts. Paid fees may resolve to refund, credit, or forfeit. Unpaid or uncollected fees may resolve only to no_fee_charged. The replacement follows the same job, entitlement, notice, and issue rules as direct creation. A rollback leaves the manual review active, and a concurrent decision cannot pass the active-fee uniqueness rule.

For a community publish-fee refund decision, link the approved Refund to the AdminFinancialOutcome and enqueue its provider job in the decision transaction. Keep applied_status pending and applied_by_user_id null until Stripe confirms success; a definite failure sets applied_status failed. For a publish-credit decision, keep the HostPublishEntitlement creation, applied outcome, and notice in one local transaction; add uniqueness on its non-null source financial outcome so a replay cannot create a second entitlement. Because one HostPublishEntitlement is an indivisible replacement for one paid publish fee, create it only after the full-fee validation above. An admin retry of a failed publish-fee refund returns its linked outcome to pending under the publish-fee lock order above and is blocked by any sibling decision whose applied_status is pending, applied, or not_applicable, including manual_review and forfeit. Use the same active-decision predicate as creation; a sibling need not have returned or reserved value to block cash retry. For existing direct/admin refund records, retain the refund eligibility, reason, audit, and step-up checks, but make retry create a durable attempt against the same refund identity. A repeated admin idempotency key returns the current detail.

The admin decision and its provider application have separate immutable audit meanings. At a queued cash-refund decision, record create_financial_outcome as succeeded and apply_financial_outcome as pending in the same transaction as the approved Refund and job. A definite initiating failure with no job records apply_financial_outcome as failed. Synchronous credit and forfeit application retain succeeded. Later Stripe results change AdminFinancialOutcome and RefundEvent, never the original AdminAction; they do not create a synthetic worker-as-admin action. Read surfaces show the original pending disposition alongside current financial state.

A later active decision for the same HostPublishFee supersedes every earlier definitely failed or cancelled cash-refund decision without changing those Refund rows or their immutable audits. In the replacement-decision transaction, query all earlier failed or cancelled Refund and AdminFinancialOutcome rows for the fee, then lock their Refund, outcome, and MoneyIssue rows in stable UUID order after the fee's normal payment and fee locks. Update every open issue, whether it previously recommended retry_refund or recover_provider_reference, to the narrow review_superseding_financial_outcome recommendation and append a MoneyIssueEvent whose metadata identifies the replacement AdminFinancialOutcome and whose admin_action_id identifies the initiating AdminAction. Add that action code to the finite MoneyIssue model, canonical migration, and read vocabulary; retain each original issue type because each earlier failure remains true. A pending replacement, including manual_review, keeps all earlier issues open for review and blocks every earlier cash retry. Refund-issue staging and reclassification must preserve this recommendation, and must not reopen an issue already resolved as superseded, while a conflicting active sibling remains; current stage_refund_money_issue unconditionally resets recommendations and reopens resolved issues. If the replacement later fails and no other active decision exists, recompute each earlier issue's repair recommendation separately from that Refund's current verified provider state and append a change event to each issue. An unresolved old attempt remains reserved and cannot be relabeled retry_refund.

Add the narrowly scoped MoneyIssue resolution reason superseded_by_financial_outcome to its model, canonical migration, finite rules, and API/read vocabulary rather than labeling an internal replacement handled_externally or provider_completed_no_action_required. The staff resolution validator must lock and recheck the fee's payment, fee, every earlier failed or cancelled Refund and outcome, the replacement outcome, and the selected issues in the publish-fee order and stable UUID order. Each issue must target a definitely failed/cancelled earlier Refund for the same fee, and no current or historical provider attempt for that fee may remain capable of returning additional value. The replacement must either have applied a validated full-fee credit or forfeit, or have a confirmed succeeded refund that returned the full intended cash amount. Record the replacement ID and resolving AdminAction in every MoneyIssueEvent; keep every issue open if the replacement is merely pending or not_applicable without proof that no cash obligation remains. Each earlier Refund retry validator independently rejects every active sibling decision before creating a new attempt or job, even if its issue recommendation is stale.

Credit release and restoration use the existing GameCreditUsage uniqueness and balance rules. Replace the notice helper's single credit_restored boolean with a finite committed credit component: none, released, restored, or mixed. Derive it from all committed GameCreditUsage rows for the booking. Released copy says reserved credit was released back to the available balance; restored copy says redeemed credit was restored; mixed copy uses the truthful common wording that credit was returned to the available balance. Cash-plus-credit copy uses the same distinction. A credit-only notice is created in the same transaction that commits both the credit return and the cancellation or removal entitlement change, and becomes visible on commit. When cash is pending on the same booking, the notice reports only completed credit; cash success later updates the booking notice.

A credit_release_failed or credit_restore_failed MoneyIssue created after official cancellation or player removal rolled back is an origin-workflow repair marker, not authority for the generic credit-retry service to move the ledger independently. Identify it from its finite issue type, operation key, target booking/game/usage, and recorded origin metadata. A stale preview, changed roster/booking state, ineligible usage, or concurrent completion leaves the issue open and returns no credit. Replays return the already committed result without moving value or notifying twice.

### 3.4 Focused Stripe refund job and attempt lifecycle

Multiple matches, a third page with more results, a matching candidate with mismatched monetary fields, or a contradictory historical attempt cannot prove a safe outcome and enter MoneyIssue; unrelated refunds on that charge are ignored. ## 4. Failures And Edge Cases

These cases identify when value or messaging could become false. Each handling rule either preserves an amount reservation, requires provider proof, or routes an unresolved obligation to the existing repair path.

1. **Process stops around a Stripe call**
   - **Condition:** The worker dies before the attempt-started checkpoint, after that checkpoint but before the call, during the call, or after Stripe responds but before the result commits.
   - **Required behavior:** The request key remains the one committed with the intent. A pre-checkpoint reclaim may begin the first call; a post-checkpoint reclaim reads for a matching Stripe refund before any same-key retry. Lease exhaustion stages the mapped MoneyIssue when possible; support-staging failure commits a terminal job diagnostic and a staff-visible unresolved Refund without repeated reclaim. A started ambiguous attempt keeps its amount reserved.

2. **Unknown outcome outlives safe key reuse**
   - **Condition:** No provider refund ID can be verified and the attempt-started timestamp is at least 12 hours old, or a bounded provider search cannot prove absence.
   - **Required behavior:** Stop automatic mutation, keep processing or unknown and the amount reserved, and present the refund for authorized staff reconciliation. A new admin key cannot bypass this block.

3. **Overlapping refund origins**
   - **Condition:** Compensation, cancellation, removal, publish-fee, or admin work concurrently targets the same Stripe payment.
   - **Required behavior:** Serialize on the payment, reuse an existing origin intent, account for every current reservation and unique succeeded attempt snapshot, and reject any excess before enqueue. Every definitely failed retryable origin consumes only the remaining amount; zero remainder follows the verified no-action path, and a sibling reservation defers retry.

4. **Late, duplicate, or contradictory provider event**
   - **Condition:** Stripe repeats an event, reports an older attempt, sends processing after success, or reports a mismatched charge, amount, currency, or metadata.
   - **Required behavior:** Record or deduplicate safely, preserve the confirmed value state, and stage an issue for a real conflict. A verified older success counts as returned cash and sets the automatic-mutation block. An unstarted newer attempt stops without a call; a started one remains reserved until both attempts are reconciled. Do not create a second refund row or a success notice from an invalid observation.

5. **Credit return fails or is replayed**
   - **Condition:** A release or restoration cannot commit, or an operator repeats a successful repair.
   - **Required behavior:** Preserve the originating workflow's rollback and mark the issue reexecute_origin_workflow. Refuse an independent generic ledger retry; use a fresh authorized origin preview to commit the cancellation or removal, one ledger return per original usage, issue resolution, and notice together. Replay returns the committed result without moving value or notifying twice.

6. **Provider cannot start or completes unsuccessfully**
   - **Condition:** A charge is missing, Stripe configuration is unavailable, or Stripe definitively fails or cancels a refund.
   - **Required behavior:** Do not claim cash returned. Record a safe reason and MoneyIssue, keep the obligation visible, and permit a new attempt only when no prior mutation could have returned value.

7. **One component completes before another**
   - **Condition:** A booking has credit and cash, multiple payments, or a publish-fee decision whose refund is still pending.
   - **Required behavior:** Derive summaries and notices from each committed component. A later outcome updates the same customer-facing notice without overstating an incomplete component.
